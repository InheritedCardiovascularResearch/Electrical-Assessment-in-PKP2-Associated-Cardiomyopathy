# Electrical Assessment in PKP2-Associated Cardiomyopathy

## ECG Findings, Ventricular Arrhythmias and Clinical Monitoring

## Overview

PKP2-associated arrhythmogenic cardiomyopathy (ACM) can affect both the structure of the heart and its electrical activity. Ventricular arrhythmias and ECG abnormalities are important features of the disease, and in some individuals they may become apparent before significant structural changes develop.

This document examines the electrical presentation of PKP2-associated cardiomyopathy, the investigations used to identify rhythm abnormalities and their relevance to clinical assessment. It also discusses the investigational RP-A601 gene therapy program developed by Rocket Pharmaceuticals.

## 1. PKP2 and Cardiac Electrical Function

The *PKP2* gene encodes plakophilin-2, a protein involved in cardiac desmosomes. These structures maintain connections between neighboring heart muscle cells and contribute to the mechanical integrity of the myocardium.

Pathogenic *PKP2* variants can disrupt normal cardiac tissue function and contribute to arrhythmogenic cardiomyopathy.

The disease can involve:

- Abnormal ventricular electrical activity.
- Changes in myocardial structure.
- Right ventricular dysfunction and, in some cases, left ventricular involvement.
- Ventricular arrhythmias.
- Progressive myocardial remodeling.

Electrical instability is a significant clinical feature. Its appearance and severity vary between individuals, including members of the same family who carry the same disease-associated variant.

## 2. Electrocardiographic Findings

A standard 12-lead electrocardiogram (ECG) records electrical activity from different regions of the heart. It is an important part of the assessment of suspected arrhythmogenic cardiomyopathy.

Several findings are relevant to PKP2-associated disease.

### T-wave inversion

T-wave inversion in the right precordial leads, particularly V1 to V3, is a recognized repolarization abnormality in arrhythmogenic right ventricular cardiomyopathy.

Its diagnostic significance depends on factors including age, the extent of inversion and the presence of other clinical findings.

T-wave inversion is not specific to PKP2-associated disease and cannot establish a diagnosis independently.

### Epsilon waves

Epsilon waves are small electrical signals that may appear immediately after the QRS complex in the right precordial leads.

They have historically been associated with arrhythmogenic right ventricular cardiomyopathy and appear in established diagnostic criteria.

Their detection can be difficult, and they are not present in every affected individual.

### Terminal activation delay

Delayed terminal activation reflects slower electrical activation during the final phase of ventricular depolarization.

Prolonged terminal activation in the right precordial leads can contribute to the assessment of arrhythmogenic cardiomyopathy.

### Ventricular ectopy

Ventricular ectopy refers to premature electrical activity originating in the ventricles. Its frequency and morphology can provide additional information during diagnostic evaluation.

These ECG findings must be interpreted alongside imaging, genetic results and clinical history.

## 3. Diagnostic Criteria and Clinical Interpretation

ECG abnormalities and ventricular arrhythmias contribute to established diagnostic frameworks for arrhythmogenic cardiomyopathy.

The 2010 Revised Task Force Criteria assess several categories:

- Global or regional ventricular dysfunction and structural abnormalities.
- Myocardial tissue characterization.
- Repolarization abnormalities.
- Depolarization and conduction abnormalities.
- Ventricular arrhythmias.
- Family history and genetic findings.

The criteria combine major and minor findings across these categories. A single ECG abnormality or pathogenic variant is not sufficient to establish every clinical diagnosis.

The 2020 Padua criteria expanded the assessment of arrhythmogenic cardiomyopathy to include left ventricular involvement and a broader disease phenotype.

These frameworks help clinicians distinguish between genetic findings, electrical abnormalities and clinically expressed cardiomyopathy.

## 4. Ventricular Arrhythmias

Ventricular arrhythmias are among the most important clinical concerns in PKP2-associated cardiomyopathy.

### Premature ventricular contractions

Premature ventricular contractions (PVCs) are extra heartbeats originating in the ventricles.

They may occur occasionally or frequently. Their number, morphology and pattern can contribute to the diagnostic assessment of arrhythmogenic cardiomyopathy.

### Nonsustained ventricular tachycardia

Nonsustained ventricular tachycardia (NSVT) consists of a short episode of rapid ventricular beats that terminates spontaneously.

It may occur without noticeable symptoms or cause palpitations, dizziness and light-headedness.

### Sustained ventricular tachycardia

Sustained ventricular tachycardia is a more serious rhythm disturbance that requires urgent clinical assessment.

Ventricular arrhythmias can be associated with severe complications, including sudden cardiac death. Their clinical significance depends on the wider presentation, including symptoms, ventricular function, imaging findings and family history.

An isolated arrhythmia should not be used as a complete measure of an individual's risk.

## 5. Ambulatory ECG and Rhythm Monitoring

A resting ECG records only a brief period of cardiac activity. Intermittent arrhythmias may not occur during the examination.

Ambulatory ECG monitoring provides a longer recording and helps clinicians identify rhythm abnormalities that might otherwise go undetected.

### Holter monitoring

Holter monitoring continuously records cardiac electrical activity, commonly over 24 to 48 hours.

It can help identify:

- PVC frequency and burden.
- Episodes of NSVT.
- Changes in rhythm during daily activities.
- Possible relationships between symptoms and abnormal heartbeats.

### Extended ECG monitoring

Longer monitoring may be appropriate when symptoms occur less frequently or when clinicians need additional information about intermittent arrhythmias.

Options include extended external ECG monitors, event recorders and, in selected cases, implantable loop recorders.

### Three purposes of rhythm monitoring

Ambulatory monitoring has several distinct clinical applications:

**Diagnostic assessment:** Identifying ventricular arrhythmias that contribute to the diagnosis of arrhythmogenic cardiomyopathy.

**Symptom correlation:** Determining whether palpitations, dizziness or fainting coincide with an abnormal rhythm.

**Clinical risk assessment:** Evaluating the type and frequency of ventricular arrhythmias alongside other established risk factors.

These purposes are related but not interchangeable. A recording that identifies an arrhythmia does not independently determine future clinical outcomes.

## 6. Electrical Changes Before Structural Abnormalities

Electrical and structural manifestations of arrhythmogenic cardiomyopathy do not necessarily develop at the same time.

Some individuals carrying pathogenic *PKP2* variants may show ECG abnormalities or ventricular arrhythmias before clear structural changes appear on echocardiography or cardiac MRI.

This is relevant to family screening. Relatives who carry a familial variant may have no symptoms and no obvious structural abnormalities at the time of assessment.

ECG testing and appropriate rhythm monitoring can help identify early electrical manifestations.

However, early electrical abnormalities do not mean that every carrier will develop advanced disease. Penetrance and clinical expression vary, and findings must be interpreted in the context of age, family history, genetic results and other investigations.

## 7. Combining Electrical Assessment With Cardiac Imaging

ECG and cardiac imaging provide different information about the heart.

| Investigation | What it assesses |
|---|---|
| Resting ECG | Repolarization, depolarization and conduction abnormalities |
| Holter monitoring | Ventricular ectopy and intermittent arrhythmias |
| Extended ECG monitoring | Less frequent rhythm disturbances and symptom correlation |
| Echocardiography | Ventricular dimensions, function and regional movement |
| Cardiac MRI | Ventricular structure, function and myocardial tissue characteristics |
| Genetic testing | Disease-associated variants |

Cardiac MRI can identify ventricular abnormalities and tissue changes that may not be apparent on a resting ECG. Conversely, ECG and ambulatory monitoring can reveal electrical abnormalities that imaging alone cannot establish.

Combining these investigations helps clinicians assess the different manifestations of PKP2-associated disease.

## 8. Genetic Testing and Family Screening

Genetic testing can identify pathogenic or likely pathogenic variants in *PKP2*.

When a disease-associated variant is identified, cascade testing may be offered to appropriate relatives following genetic counselling.

Clinical screening may include:

- Personal and family history.
- Resting 12-lead ECG.
- Ambulatory ECG monitoring.
- Echocardiography.
- Cardiac MRI where appropriate.
- Targeted testing for the familial genetic variant.

A relative may carry the variant without showing clinical manifestations at the time of testing. Follow-up should reflect the individual's age, family history, genetic findings and clinical assessment.

## 9. Rocket Pharmaceuticals and RP-A601

Rocket Pharmaceuticals is developing RP-A601, an investigational gene therapy for PKP2-associated arrhythmogenic cardiomyopathy.

The program uses an adeno-associated virus rh74 (AAVrh74) vector to deliver a functional human *PKP2* gene.

Its scientific rationale is based on restoring plakophilin-2 expression in relevant cardiac cells.

### Early clinical findings

On May 15, 2025, Rocket Pharmaceuticals reported preliminary findings from three patients in its Phase 1 RP-A601 clinical trial.

The company reported:

- Increased PKP2 protein expression.
- Improved localization of proteins involved in desmosomal structure.
- Improvement or stabilization in arrhythmia burden.
- Improvement or stabilization in cardiac function and quality of life.

These findings were reported by the company and involved only three patients. They provide preliminary information about biological activity and clinical observations, not definitive evidence of efficacy.

Further clinical data and longer follow-up are needed to determine whether the reported findings are sustained and translate into meaningful patient benefit.

### Why electrical assessment matters to RP-A601

Arrhythmias are a significant feature of PKP2-associated cardiomyopathy. ECG findings and ambulatory monitoring therefore provide important information when assessing the clinical presentation of the disease.

For gene therapy research, several outcomes need to remain distinct:

- Gene delivery and PKP2 protein expression.
- Changes in desmosomal protein localization.
- Ventricular structure and function.
- Frequency and characteristics of arrhythmias.
- Symptoms and broader clinical outcomes.

Evidence of gene delivery or increased protein expression does not establish that ventricular arrhythmias have been prevented or that long-term clinical outcomes have improved.

Each outcome requires appropriate clinical evaluation.

## 10. Important Distinctions When Interpreting the Evidence

- A pathogenic *PKP2* variant does not determine an individual's clinical severity.
- ECG abnormalities are not exclusive to PKP2-associated disease.
- A normal resting ECG does not exclude intermittent arrhythmias.
- Electrical abnormalities may appear before visible structural changes.
- PVC frequency and NSVT require interpretation alongside other clinical findings.
- Increased PKP2 protein expression is evidence of biological activity, not proof of clinical efficacy.
- Company-reported preliminary findings should not be presented as independently established clinical outcomes.
- Electrical, structural and functional measures should be evaluated separately.
